About

I currently work at Qualcomm, where I have been since September 2026 as an Associate Engineer in Bangalore. I'm part of the automotive systems team, and my work is optimizing the performance of ML and GenAI models on Qualcomm chipsets.

I finished my B.E. in Computer Science at BITS Pilani, Goa Campus, in 2026. During my degree I researched video anomaly detection in capsule endoscopy with collaborators at NTNU and APPCAIR, and graph and language models for drug-target interaction prediction. I also interned at Carrier Technologies, KrakenTech and IndiaAI.

My interests span machine learning, computer vision and reinforcement learning. What draws me most is putting these ideas to work: taking ML and generative AI beyond the benchmark and into systems that solve real problems.

Research

  1. Aug 2025 to Jul 2026

    Video anomaly detection in wireless capsule endoscopy

    APPCAIR (BITS Pilani) and NTNU, with Prof. Ashwin Srinivasan, Prof. Tanmay Tulsidas Verlekar and Dr. Anuja Vats

    Unsupervised and weakly supervised anomaly detection on medical video. Patch-level early fusion of RGB and optical flow reached 92.50% accuracy and 92.15% F1. Intermediate fusion with Endo-FM blocks and CNN pyramids reached 91.9% accuracy and 91.2% F1. I also designed a multi-scale late-fusion model with dual-stream TimeSformer encoders and hierarchical cross-attention.

    Code on GitHub

  2. Apr 2025

    Multi-level graph attention network for drug-target interaction prediction

    BITS Pilani, with Dr. Hemant Rathore

    A graph attention network built on graph convolutions, trained on 306 drugs, 407 protein targets and 970 confirmed interactions. It achieved state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets.

    Code on GitHub

  3. Aug 2025

    Association rule mining enhanced BERT for drug-target interaction prediction

    BITS Pilani, with Dr. Hemant Rathore

    A hybrid of association rule mining and BERT over protein sequences and SMILES. Rules mined from positive interactions mark relevant subsequences, which improved interpretability and performance. Training used mixed precision and class-balanced losses for imbalanced data.

    Code on GitHub
